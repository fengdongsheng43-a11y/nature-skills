# Falsification and novelty audit

## Purpose

Test whether the proposed contribution survives the strongest reasonable skeptical interpretation.

## 1. Decompose the innovation

Split the idea into minimum units, for example:

- material or organism;
- process or synthesis route;
- representation;
- trigger or feedback;
- mechanism;
- measurement;
- causal claim;
- optimization strategy;
- application boundary;
- scale-up or deployment constraint.

A project can be new in one unit and conventional in the others.

## 2. Define the nearest baseline

Choose the strongest credible baseline, not the easiest control.

The baseline should answer: what would a skeptical expert use instead if the proposed contribution did not exist?

## 3. Remove the labels

Restate the proposed contribution without branding, material names, fashionable terminology, or application packaging.

Ask whether the remaining causal or mechanistic delta is still meaningful.

If the contribution disappears after relabeling, the innovation is likely narrative rather than scientific.

## 4. Search prior art by component

Search mechanism, material, trigger, process, measurement, and application separately and in combinations.

Record what was searched. Do not convert incomplete search coverage into universal absence.

## 5. Run reviewer attacks

For each central claim ask:

- Is this already known in another material or system?
- Is the improvement caused by a simpler uncontrolled variable?
- Does the proposed mechanism add explanatory power beyond performance data?
- Is the new component necessary?
- Does the evidence distinguish the mechanism from the strongest rival?
- Would the claim survive if the target application changed?
- Is the contribution broad enough to matter beyond one recipe or one dataset?

## 6. Innovation vector

Use a structured judgment rather than a single score.

Suggested dimensions:

- `conceptual novelty`;
- `mechanistic gain`;
- `discriminability`;
- `experimental feasibility`;
- `generality/significance`.

Report each as `strong`, `moderate`, `weak`, `unclear`, or `contradicted` with evidence notes. Do not fabricate numerical precision.

## 7. Verdict language

Use one of:

- `survives current audit`;
- `survives only after narrowing`;
- `not yet assessable`;
- `likely incremental under current evidence`;
- `not worth pursuing under current constraints`.

A positive verdict remains conditional on the documented evidence boundary.
