## 3. Provisional substantive signals

**Signal A: atomic precision + transport/architecture + implementation**
is a demonstrably existing combination, not a brand-new conceptual pairing.
Strong nearest comparable works: Nature Water 2025 ceramic membrane;
Nature Water 2026 kilogram synthesis; AFM 2025 MXene membrane.
Allowed: "Scale and functional-material integration already have
high-level publication precedents."
Forbidden: "This trend grew by x%" or "all top journals require scale-up."

**Signal B: pathway selectivity beyond simple degradation efficiency**
is visible in the checked works. ES&T 2024 selective H* dechlorination;
AFM 2025 high-valent Ru polymerization; WR 2026 oxidant-free electron
transfer; ES&T 2026 electro-enhanced PMS. This makes a generic
SAC + PMS + high removal% proposition vulnerable to nearest-prior-art
challenge. No validated saturation conclusion yet.

**Signal C: mechanistic identifiability is consequential.** The
2024 ES&T critical review and 2024 challenge to indiscriminate
singlet-oxygen interpretations make a single scavenger/EPR result
inadequate for an unqualified pathway-dominance claim. Exact controls
depend on the proposed system. Competing pathways and measurement
artifacts must be explicitly handled.

**Signal D: data-driven materials reasoning exists as published
water-research work** (WR 2026 paper), but it is not proof that
LLM-guided screening is generally reliable or newly popular across
all four journals.

**Signal E: Nature Water explicitly published an editorial titled
"Clarifying our remit as we enter a new phase" on 2026-01-23
(DOI 10.1038/s44221-026-00588-y), but its full editorial body was
not available in this retrieval. Do not guess the clarified exclusions.

## 4. Serious rivals and provisional death conditions

### H1: A novel atom-site environment controls genuinely different
reaction selectivity in realistic wastewater.

- Rival R1: apparent improvement reflects site density, adsorption,
  support conductivity/porosity, pH, residual clusters or leached metals,
  not the asserted isolated-site electronic mechanism.
- Rival R2: selected probe/scavenger and EPR conditions misattribute
  1O2, radicals or electron transfer.
- Discriminator: matched metal/site loadings and support-only, leachate,
  homogeneous-metal, particle/cluster controls; before/during/after
  metal coordination structure; pathway-specific orthogonal kinetics
  and probes with matrix-matched blanks and carbon/product balance.
- Death condition: rival material reproduces both selectivity and
  rates after credible normalization; or claimed stable single-atom
  sites are absent at relevant operating time; or route remains
  non-identifiable after valid orthogonal controls.
- State: hypothesis active; communication claim evidence-incomplete.

### H2: New catalyst configuration is meaningfully superior for deployment.

- Rival: gain vanishes after matrix effects, oxidant/energy input,
  membrane resistance, toxicity, deactivation or cost constraints.
- Discriminator: realistic influent controls, time-on-stream and
  continuous flow, chemical/energy balance, transformation products,
  leaching and spent catalyst structure under matched operating targets.
- Death condition: credible practical baseline meets or exceeds
  performance under same safety and resource constraints.
- State: not assessable without project-specific data and baseline.

## 5. Provisional journal-role interpretation (not editor intent)

- AFM observed example centers structure–site–interface–function.
- ES&T examples center environmental transformation pathways,
  measurement validity and relevant exposure media.
- Water Research examples integrate oxidation mechanism with reactor,
  operating matrix or structured decision methods.
- Nature Water examples demonstrate cross-scale/waste-resource/
  engineering contribution narratives.

These are **selected examples, not exclusive journal profiles**.
Matched full corpora and held-out counterexample tests are required before
describing a journal-specific preference or evidence-standard shift.
